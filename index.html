HumanLean

Faithful Lean proofs, made for human review

Yuhao Mao, Chenhao Sun, Yani Zhang, and Martin Vechev

AI-generated mathematics is increasingly accompanied by AI-generated Lean formalizations. Existing formalization agents optimize for compilation, but compilation alone does not guarantee faithfulness to the original argument, and unfaithfulness undermines the correctness guarantee that formalization is meant to provide. We introduce HumanLean, the first system designed to produce compiling, faithful Lean proofs for human review.

Unfaithfulness is widespread

We found widespread unfaithfulness in formalizations from OpenAI’s recent advances, Axiom’s Stacks project, and various projects listed on Papers with Lean. This significantly undermines the correctness guarantees for the original mathematics.

Easing human review of formalizations

Unguided faithfulness

With HumanLean (powered by GPT-6-Astra), we observed 100% faithfulness in both statements and proofs across our evaluation of 12 publications and the unit-distance proof without human guidance. High unguided faithfulness reduces the chance of human intervention and thus reduces expected workload.

Compositional formalization

HumanLean proves internal results and exposes external results as explicit axioms. This division is more natural and manageable for human readers.

Minimal formalization

Compared with a raw agent instructed to do the same compositional formalization, HumanLean uses roughly 90% fewer custom definitions per formalized goal across both benchmark models, with fewer axioms and duplicate encodings. A minimal formalization reduces reading difficulty and improves reusability.

Interestingly, the stronger Astra model produces more definitions and duplicates per formalized goal in the raw-agent baseline. In HumanLean, it reduces verbosity, producing fewer definitions, proof declarations and duplicates per goal.

Example: a faithful unit-distance proof formalization

A formalization can change a statement, replace an argument, or assume a result the original proof sets out to establish. Thus, an unfaithful formalization cannot establish the correctness of the original proof, even if it compiles.

We formalize the unit-distance proof by OpenAI and compare our formalization with three other publicly available AI-assisted formalizations (plby, Logical Intelligence, and fbundle).

Importantly, we found all three compared formalizations unfaithful to the original proof.
